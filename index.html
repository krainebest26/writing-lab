Show, Don't Tell: the ABSDT camera

A telling sentence gives the reader a fact. A showing sentence lets the reader see it and work out the feeling. Examiners give the most Content marks to the moment you slow down and show. Five letters help you do it.

Telling (Band 3)

I found a wallet with a lot of money and I was tempted to keep it.

Showing (Band 4 to 5)

A thick stack of red hundred-dollar notes stared back at me. My breath caught in my throat. My fingers tightened around the wallet.

Step 8 · Situational writing

Situational writing: every point, in the right voice

Situational writing is the first part of Paper 1, worth 14 marks: 6 for Task Fulfilment (every content point, the right purpose and reader) and 8 for Language and Organisation (tone, paragraphs, grammar). No ABSDT here. Clear, polite and complete is what scores.

PPurpose. Why am I writing? To ask, invite, report, thank or apologise. Say it in your first paragraph.
AAudience. A teacher, principal or stranger means formal. A friend or family member means informal.
CContent points. Usually 6. Some come from the task. Some you must make up yourself. Each point gets its own sentence or paragraph.
